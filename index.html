01 · Financial LLMs

From domain models to finance-focused LLMs

This timeline follows selected models trained or adapted for financial language and tasks. It distinguishes encoder-based domain models from generative LLMs and open adaptation frameworks; the models are not directly comparable by size or benchmark score.

2019–21

FinBERT brings domain adaptation to financial text

FinBERT adapts BERT to financial language, initially targeting tasks such as financial sentiment analysis. Follow-on variants explore additional financial corpora and pretraining strategies. This is the specialist-model foundation before the generative LLM wave.

BERT-based encoderFinancial NLP

2022

FLANG extends financial-domain pretraining

FLANG explores domain-adaptive language modeling for financial NLP and contributes to the move from task-specific sentiment models toward broader financial text understanding.

Domain-adaptive pretrainingPre-generative LLM era

2023

BloombergGPT demonstrates large-scale finance-focused pretraining

Bloomberg introduces a 50-billion-parameter model trained on a mixture of financial and general-purpose data. Its paper describes a 363-billion-token financial dataset, augmented with 345 billion public-dataset tokens, and evaluates both financial and general NLP tasks.

BloombergGPT · 50B parametersMixed financial + general corpus
Read the BloombergGPT paper ↗  ·  Bloomberg announcement ↗
2023

Open financial LLM development accelerates: FinGPT and FinMA

FinGPT emphasizes open, data-centric pipelines for collecting and curating financial data, with parameter-efficient adaptation such as LoRA. FinMA explores instruction-tuned financial models built on open foundation models. The emphasis shifts toward reproducible adaptation and task-specific assistants, rather than only training a very large model from scratch.

Open model ecosystemInstruction tuning · LoRA
FinGPT paper ↗  ·  PIXIU / FinMA paper ↗
2024

Open FinLLMs diversify across backbones, languages and tasks

Financial adaptations based on Llama-family and other open models proliferate, including FinLlama, FinTral, InvestLM and multilingual variants. The field becomes less about one monolithic finance model and more about adapting foundation models to specific datasets, languages and use cases.

Multiple foundation-model familiesTask- and language-specific
Read the FinLLMs survey ↗  ·  Financial LLM applications survey ↗
2024–26

Evaluation and governance become part of the FinLLM lifecycle

Research attention expands beyond releasing financial LLMs toward systematic evaluation, readiness and governance. Open financial-LLM leaderboard efforts aim to compare models across defined financial task families. This is a shift in the ecosystem—not evidence that one financial model has become universally best.

Evaluation and leaderboard effortsGovernance and readiness
See the NeurIPS FinBen benchmark paper ↗
What changed? The progression is not simply “bigger models.” It runs from domain-specific encoders, to large financial pretraining (BloombergGPT), to open and parameter-efficient adaptation (FinGPT and FinMA), and then to a broader ecosystem of task-specific models, evaluation and governance. In banking, a financial model still needs current approved data, controlled retrieval, rigorous validation and human oversight.
02 · CFA progress

From exam questions to advanced reasoning

Published studies use mock CFA material. They are useful measures of selected financial knowledge and reasoning—not official CFA Institute exam results or professional credentials.

2023

GPT-4 shows promise at Level I, with a drop at Level II

An early study tested GPT models on mock Level I and II questions. GPT-4 performed strongly on Level I, while Level II proved more difficult. The paper did not test Level III.

First CFA-style evaluationsLevel III not tested
Read the 2023 study ↗
2024

All three levels enter the research conversation

An EMNLP Industry Track study evaluated proprietary and open models across Levels I–III. Leading proprietary models could pass the mock Level I and II tests, but Level III—especially constructed-response questions—remained difficult.

All three levels assessedEssay / constructed response matters
Read the EMNLP paper ↗
2025

Reasoning models make a substantial step at Level III

A study of 23 models tested mock Level III material, including multiple-choice and constructed-response questions. It reported a 79.1% composite score for o4-mini and 77.3% for Gemini 2.5 Flash under its revised essay-grading methodology.

o4-mini · 79.1%Gemini 2.5 Flash · 77.3%
Read the Level III study ↗
2025–26

Cross-level tests add retrieval and newer reasoning models

Further studies examine retrieval-augmented generation across all three levels and evaluate newer reasoning models on mock exams. Results are promising, but differ in question sets, prompts, grading and pass criteria; they should not be merged into one league table.

RAG and curriculum retrievalStudy-specific results
All-level RAG study ↗  ·  Cross-level reasoning study ↗
What changed? The research focus moved from “Can an LLM answer CFA questions?” toward “Can reasoning, retrieval and structured workflows solve harder professional tasks reliably?” Mock-exam performance is evidence of capability on those tests—not proof of professional competence.
03 · Financial agents

From a chatbot to a digital analyst team

An agent combines a language model with instructions, specialist skills, data connectors and tools. It can coordinate multiple steps and prepare work for human review.

2024

Financial multi-agent research becomes more concrete

Research frameworks such as FinRobot explore financial agents for tasks including market analysis and investment research. This is an early bridge between financial LLM research and multi-step applications.

Explore FinRobot ↗
2025–26

Anthropic publishes a practical Financial Services agent toolkit

Anthropic's public repository brings together named workflow agents, reusable skills, slash commands and data connectors. It can be used as a Cowork plugin or deployed through the Claude Managed Agents API.

Explore Anthropic Financial Services on GitHub ↗
A real-world example

Claude for Financial Services

Instead of one generic assistant, the repository offers workflow-specific agents. Each bundles a role, skills and access to relevant tools. The outputs are drafts for qualified human review—not autonomous financial decisions.

Pitch AgentComps, precedents and LBO work assembled into a pitch deck.
Market ResearcherIndustry overview, competitive landscape and peer comparisons.
Earnings ReviewerEarnings calls and filings turned into model updates and draft notes.
Model BuilderDCF, LBO, three-statement and comparable-company models.
GL ReconcilerFinds breaks, traces causes and routes items for sign-off.
KYC ScreenerParses onboarding documents and flags missing information.
Why it matters for banking: the repository is a reference architecture, not a ready-made IRRBB solution. It demonstrates how to package expertise into skills and agents. A bank could apply the same pattern to an NII/EVE challenge partner—with approved ALM outputs, model documentation, validation findings and strict human oversight.
04 · Benchmarking

How financial AI progress is measured

Benchmarks turn broad claims into defined tests. Each one measures a slice of capability; no single score captures “financial intelligence.”
